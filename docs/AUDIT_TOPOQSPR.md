# TopoQSPR v0.x audit (October 2026)

Expert panel of seven independent reviewers (chemical graph theory, ChEMBL data curation, QSPR/ML methodology, research software engineering, 2026 landscape, and two red teams attacking the lead author's paper and strategy), followed by an adversarial skeptic who tried to reproduce or refute every critical/major finding. All work was done on 2026-10-02 against commit `912ea67`-era code and ChEMBL 37 (release 2026-05-01).

Skeptic outcome: **41 upheld, 6 refuted (mostly partly), 6 uncertain** out of 53 items re-tested. Raw records: [`audit/expert-reports-2026-10.json`](audit/expert-reports-2026-10.json), [`audit/skeptic-2026-10.json`](audit/skeptic-2026-10.json).

## Key conflicts resolved by the skeptic

- ChemGraphX citation: landscape-2026 says refs.bib has 19 entries and does not cite ChemGraphX; redteam-strategy says refs.bib:128 cites it and main.tex:70 positions it. Resolved: the draft was edited at 16:38 on 2026-10-02 and now has 24 entries including ChemGraphX and DOPtools. The landscape finding is stale. Several experts' main.tex line numbers are also off by about 3.
- SoftwareX word limit: redteam-paper says about 3,000; redteam-strategy says 4,000. Secondary sources (search snippets of the SoftwareX guide) say 4,000, excluding title, authors, references and metadata tables. The primary page returned 403, so this needs manual confirmation.
- Value of the indices over size: redteam-strategy says TI-135 is worse than 2 size counts on ESOL; qspr-ml says gains are small (+0.05/+0.07). My runs show both claims depend on the learner and on n. ESOL: Ridge TI 0.20 < size 0.34, but RF TI 0.53 > size 0.40. Approved drugs at n=800: TI beats size by +0.14 (LogP) and +0.17 (TPSA). The robust statement is that composition counts beat TI by a wide margin in every setting.
- qspr-ml rates the shared LinearRegression bug as corrupting best models. In a realistic run LinearReg scores R² ≈ -1e9 (n<p) and never wins, so the stored models are unaffected. The mechanism is real; the practical severity is low.
- software-eng: /validate takes 105-221 s and a first analysis 'cannot start'. My measurements: 15-44 s cold, and 0.5 s on retry because the chembl client's HTTP cache is warm, so the UI's retry message works.
- software-eng: PubChem failures cached as {} 'poison the cache permanently'. The code's `if cached:` treats {} as a miss, so this is refuted.
- Hosoya prevalence: 73% (graph-theory, N=1,959) vs 76.6% (mine, N=2,148), from different de-duplication. Multi-fragment counts: 474/1,959 vs 1,163/3,311 (mine includes salt records). Same conclusion either way.
- C2 and C10 verdicts: the experts agree with each other but partly refute the lead (no 'all cancers' pull; family labels wrong). The whitelist zero-row count is 17 (curation) vs 18 (software-eng).
- ChemGraphX size: landscape says 150 indices; the paper's own table sums to 149 (52+52+24+21) while its text says 150.
- Kara et al. 2025 HAC R²: strategy quotes 0.998, which is the cubic model; the linear model is 0.999.

## Not covered by any expert (open items)

- Data licensing: ChEMBL is CC BY-SA 3.0. Redistributing drug sets, cached ChEMBL-derived files or result workbooks needs attribution and share-alike, and that must be stated in the paper and README alongside the MIT code licence. Nobody covered this.
- Provenance of the non-standard formulas: SS_ABC/AZ/ABS, RABC, ABC_SC (ml.py:302, sqrt((s-2)/p)/sqrt(s), an unexplained hybrid) and n+1-d come from scripts that are not in the repo (degreessum_computation.py, degree_reverse_computation*.py, ml.py:4-6). Before 'fixing' them, get those scripts and the intended definitions from the Arockiaraj group. Some may be deliberate variants used in the group's published papers.
- Feasibility of the Hosoya fix: exact Z is #P-hard in general. A frontier-memoised DP handled imatinib (41 bonds, Z ≈ 7.9e7) instantly, but nobody tested peptides and macrocycles (hundreds of bonds) or set a size cap / applicability rule.

## Research-landscape analyst (2024-2026 tools, literature, venues, name, opportunities)

The biggest finding is that the index engine is no longer new, and the people behind the closest tool are the planned co-authors. ChemGraphX (Jacob K, Clement J, Arockiaraj M, Jindal A; J. Cheminform. 2026, DOI 10.1186/s13321-025-01141-x, accepted 2025-12-11) is an open-source MIT web tool from Prof. Arockiaraj's own group. It computes 150 degree, neighbourhood-degree-sum and distance indices plus entropies. The SoftwareX draft (paper/main.tex) cites neither it nor any other descriptor or QSPR tool. Those tools include Mordred, PaDEL, alvaDesc, MathChem, PyTIE, QSPRpred, the 2026 JCIM 'QSAR in the Browser', and a Python drug-to-index pipeline by Arockiaraj's Turkish co-authors. The novelty claim therefore has to move away from '135 indices' and onto three things none of these tools do: a curated, reproducible disease-to-drug manifest from ChEMBL indications; leakage-free nested CV with a mandatory molecular-size baseline; and a large benchmark across indications. The literature makes the need clear. Europe PMC hits for 'topological indices' AND QSPR AND drugs rose from 16 (2022) to 40, 62 and 113 (2023-2025), and 77 so far in 2026, with machine learning mentioned in about half of the 2025-26 papers. Typical studies use 9-50 drugs, report in-sample or LOOCV R2, and often model properties that are themselves computed from structure. One 2026 bipolar-drug paper says ML performance 'deteriorated under leave-one-out cross-validation because of the limited sample size'. Classic critiques to cite are Dearden-Cronin-Kaiser 2009 and Gutman 2012/Furtula 2013 on index quality, plus the Ash/Walters 2025 JCIM protocol for method comparison. I checked the code against Mordred: TopoQSPR's M1, M2, ABC, Randić, Wiener and Balaban J match Mordred exactly on 5 drugs. Hosoya Z is badly wrong for larger molecules: donepezil gives 264 against a true 965,380 (C7 confirmed). On venues, SoftwareX survives my attempt to refute it. JOSS is ruled out for now because the repo has only been public since 2026-10-02 and JOSS needs six months of public history. J. Cheminform. has just published the group's overlapping ChemGraphX. SoftwareX accepts up to 4000 words and 6 figures and has recent cheminformatics precedent (scikit-fingerprints 2024, CombinatorixPy 2025). The name 'TopoQSPR' is free on PyPI, conda, the web and in Crossref; 'MolecuLens' collides with moleculens.com and at least 10 GitHub repos.

## Chemical graph theory expert: audit of ml.py compute_topological_indices (formulas, graph handling, redundancy, references)

I tested ml.py two ways. First against closed forms on paths P3–P30, cycles C4–C20, stars, benzene and naphthalene: 427 checks pass and 5 fail, all of them Hosoya Z. Second against my own networkx reference implementation and Mordred-community 2.0.7 on real drugs. For connected molecules, all the core degree and distance indices are correct: M1, M2, F, R, H, SC, GA, ABC, AZI, SO, ISI, sigma, W, J, Sz, PI, Mostar, edge-Szeged, edge-Wiener, Schultz/degree-distance and Gutman. Five defects make some indices wrong: (1) Hosoya Z is wrong for every molecule with more than 18 bonds (C7 confirmed). On 1,959 ChEMBL approved small molecules, 518 (26%) get Z=1 and 905 (46%) get a made-up formula; only 27% are correct. (2) SS_ABC, SS_AZ and SS_ABS, plus the reverse RABC, carry an extra product term in the numerator (S_u+S_v+S_uS_v-2 instead of S_u+S_v-2). They match no published definition. (3) The 9 'R*' reverse indices use rd(v)=n+1-d(v), not Kulli's c(v)=Δ-d(v)+1 that the paper states. As a result RM1, RM2 and RBM are exact polynomials in n, m, M1 and M2. (4) Balaban J uses cyclomatic number m-n+1 regardless of components, so it is wrong for every disconnected (salt) SMILES. fetcher.py passes ChEMBL canonical_smiles unstripped, and 24% of approved drugs are multi-fragment. (5) R4_* is ill-defined for drugs with Δ=3: the mod-Δ rule maps pendant vertices to 0 and the code silently drops those edges, which happens in 55% of drugs. Redundancy is large. 8 pairs are exact duplicates, 2 more are exactly proportional (one is the identity Σ(S_u+S_v)=2·M2), and at least 13 indices are exact linear or polynomial functions of others. On approved drugs, 77 of 135 indices have \|Spearman ρ\|>0.95 with heavy-atom count, and 5 principal components carry 95% of the variance. The family counts in C10 add up to 135, but the paper's 'neighbourhood degree-sum 35' row includes 5 DS_ indices that are plain-degree edge functions (4 of them duplicates), and graph entropy is listed under distance-based in config.py. The generalized R_k with the mod-Δ rule comes from the collaborator's own group (Arockiaraj, Greeni, Kalaam, IJQC 2023; Kalaam et al., Front. Chem. 2024), so it is cite-able. It needs k restricted to 1..Δ and a stated precedence for 'mod'. I recommend about 35 deduplicated, correctly defined, unit-tested indices with primary references, cross-validated against Mordred where they overlap. The paper's sentence 'checked against published reference values' is currently untrue.

## Red team: attacking the lead author's publication strategy (framing, venue, headline metric, targets/panel, naming/licence/authorship, timeline)

Most of the strategy holds up, but the evidence calls for four changes. First, the senior co-author's group already published ChemGraphX, a topological-index web tool (J Cheminform 18:39, 2026; Jacob, Clement, Arockiaraj, Jindal; Crossref-verified). TopoQSPR has to be framed as the study and evaluation layer that sits on top of index engines like ChemGraphX, and it should cross-check its values against ChemGraphX. It should not be pitched as another index calculator. Second, I ran ridge regression with repeated 5-fold CV on ChEMBL phase-4 parent drugs for four diseases (n=37 to 102, pooled 235) and on measured ESOL solubility (n=1128). Across these sets, ml.py's 135 indices add almost nothing over a 2-feature size baseline (heavy atoms and bonds). Simple element counts beat the indices by a wide margin (pooled TPSA: TI 0.36 vs composition 0.83; ESOL measured logS: TI 0.29, size 0.34, composition 0.72, RDKit 2D 0.89). So the delta-R2 over size alone is too weak a headline: it needs a composition rung and a stated upper reference. Third, computed targets (Crippen logP, TPSA, MR, MolWt) are atom-contribution sums. Modelling them is close to circular, so the headline results must use experimental endpoints. Fourth, the MIT relicensing is legally fragile. The original repo gcap733-bit/moleculens is public with no licence; 77 of its 87 commits are by gcap733-bit, 5 are by an 'Antigravity AI' agent, and the MIT licence was added by Singati2 on 2026-10-02 into a repo that is already public. Copyright stays with the original contributors until they agree. SoftwareX is still the right venue (JOSS fails on development history and web-app scope; J Cheminform would look like the group's second tool paper and needs a benchmark). The 'real quick' timeline is not realistic: a minimum credible v1.0 is about 4 to 6 weeks of focused work, then 2 to 4 months of review.

## Senior research-software engineer: web tool readiness and SoftwareX release engineering

The live site at https://moleculens.onrender.com is up: /health answered in 0.9 s and the served index.html is byte-identical to the repo. As a public scientific tool it is not ready. For any disease not already in the ChEMBL client cache, /validate took 105-221 s on the live site (asthma 221 s, hypertension 176 s, cold diabetes 105 s), but the browser gives up after 15 s (index.html:451). On a first query the UI therefore shows 'Server is still starting up' instead of running. 18 of the 73 curated disease names (25%) return zero ChEMBL rows and are hard-blocked, including 'breast cancer', 'type 2 diabetes', 'rheumatoid arthritis' and 'COPD'. Fetch strategies 2 and 3 are dead code: drug_indication is 'not searchable' in the ChEMBL client. Jobs live in an in-memory dict on a free instance that Render can restart at any time and spins down after 15 minutes idle. One local run with 37 drugs (gout) did not finish within 50 minutes wall time (132 CPU-minutes on a shared 16-core machine). That makes the advertised 20-45 min, and any run on a free tier, implausible. The API accepts any max_drugs, including 10^9, negative values and null. Both caches are keyed on the disease name only, so whoever runs a disease first fixes the result everyone else gets until restart. Failed indices silently become 0: Balaban J for metformin HCl is 0.0, while metformin gives 3.38. Dependencies are completely unpinned, with no tests, CI, release, DOI or Dockerfile. The paper's code-metadata table and Methods describe several things that do not exist yet: a run record, re-execution from that record, pinned requirements, a Docker image, nested CV and unit-tested indices. Path traversal is not exploitable today, and XSS risk is low because disease names are whitelisted and SMILES and ChEMBL IDs contain no markup. Both depend on the whitelist and need defensive fixes before the disease input is opened up. Required for SoftwareX: a tagged v1.0.0 with a Zenodo DOI, an installable package with a CLI, pinned environment plus Dockerfile, a pytest suite with CI, a provenance run record, docs, and a UI and paper that match the code. A durable job queue and hosted compute are nice-to-have. For a free web demo, run precomputed results from a tagged release and leave heavy compute to the CLI and Docker image.

## QSPR/QSAR ML methodology expert (OECD validation, nested CV, applicability domain, Y-randomisation, SHAP with collinear features)

I audited ml.py (run_correlation, run_ml_qspr, compute_shap) and the config.py target list, and ran an experiment on real data. The dataset was ChEMBL 37 phase-4-for-indication drugs for "hypertension" plus "diabetes": 209 IDs, 160 unique largest-fragment structures, 154 with MW below 1000. Indices were computed by importing the repo's own compute_topological_indices, and the repo's run_ml_qspr was also called directly. The main result is negative for the paper's story. For MolWt and MolMR, 135 indices are no better than a linear model on heavy-atom count plus bond count (nested R² 0.957 vs 0.958, and 0.973 vs 0.967). For LogP and TPSA the gain over that size baseline is small (+0.05 and +0.07) and absolute R² is low (0.33 and 0.26). The repo's tune-on-all-data-then-CV protocol is methodologically wrong (C5 confirmed in code), but here its measured bias is small (about ±0.04). That is smaller than the split-to-split variance. A bigger problem is that it reports a single seed-42 split with fold-mean R². On the unfiltered set this gave TPSA Ridge R² = −66.8, while nested CV with other seeds gave 0.80, because four peptides (insulin detemir, exenatide, pramlintide, liraglutide) dominate whichever fold they land in. I found three new bugs that are worse than C5. (1) A shared module-level LinearRegression instance is never cloned, so the stored "best model" for one property gets silently refit on the next property. Shown empirically: the MolWt model ends up predicting with r = −0.98 to MolWt. (2) VIF returns 999 for 132/135 indices, and the index matrix has numerical rank 48–61, so the VIF output carries no information. (3) KernelExplainer with nsamples=50 on 135 features keeps only 10 non-zero features per row and is unseeded. Its top-10 overlaps exact linear SHAP on only 4–6 of 10 features, and two seeds disagree on 4 of the top 10. The indices are also blind to element and bond order. Hexane, 1-pentanol, pentylamine and 1,4-butanediol get identical values for all 135 indices, yet their TPSA runs from 0 to 40.5 and their LogP from 2.59 to −0.25. So TPSA, HBD, HBA and LogP cannot be identified from these features beyond their correlation with size. Taken together, most of the current targets are either duplicates of mass, counts the graph already encodes, or properties computed by RDKit or PubChem rather than measured. v1.0 should switch to experimental endpoints (PubChem experimental annotations, AqSolDB, OPERA/PHYSPROP, the Bradley melting-point set, TDC/Biogen). It should also use repeated nested CV, mandatory size baselines with ΔR²_size as the headline number, Y-scrambling, an applicability domain, FDR control and family-level SHAP. The paper draft already claims nested CV, baselines and salt normalisation (main.tex:30, 135-136), and none of these is in the code.
